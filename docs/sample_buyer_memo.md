### 1. Intercontinental Exchange, Inc. (ICE)

**Fit: 4/5.** ICE's 10-K says it acquired a digital asset custody business in May 2025, now ICE Digital Trust, a New York limited purpose trust company supervised by NYDFS that custodies Bitcoin, Ethereum and USD Coin, so custody is already an operating line for ICE. The M&A section says ICE intends to keep pursuing acquisitions to enter new markets or related asset classes, which supports adding a US custody target, though the filing does not single out crypto custody as a stated priority. At a $500M to $1.5B deal size, the cash position is the constraint: reported cash of $936M covers 1.87x of the low end and 0.62x of the high end, so the high end would likely need debt or equity funding.

**Strategic gap addressed:** The target would add institutional digital asset custody capacity and client relationships to the ICE Digital Trust platform, which the filing describes as an early-stage business with an evolving model.

**Ability to pay:** 0.5%-1.6% of mkt cap; cash covers 0.6x-1.9x.

**Key risks:**
- The 10-K flags custody-specific risks, including loss of private keys, hacking, and possible customer liability beyond insurance coverage, which a target's control environment would need to address before integration.
- ICE Digital Trust is already subject to NYDFS virtual currency, cybersecurity, AML and capital rules, and the filing says this adds regulatory burden and cost; the target would bring its own compliance and licensing diligence.
- ICE has $24.0B of total debt against $936M of cash, so a deal at the high end ($1.5B) cannot be funded from cash alone, and ICE's stated integration record on Black Knight and its Bakkt and Polymarket investments adds execution risk.
- The filing says ICE may not realize expected synergies from acquisitions, and it is still integrating Black Knight under an FTC consent order, which may limit management bandwidth for a second crypto integration.
